What is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers impersonate trusted organizations or legitimate individuals to trick victims into revealing sensitive corporate or personal data.

Vectors of Phishing

Email Phishing

Mass-scale, generic campaigns broadcasted to thousands of potential victims simultaneously.

Spear Phishing

Highly targeted spear campaigns directed at specific personnel using personalized intelligence.

Whaling

High-profile operations explicitly targeting executives, CEOs, or financial stakeholders.

Vishing

Voice-based social engineering conducted over telephone networks using spoofed caller IDs.

Smishing

SMS or text-based attacks exploiting mobile communication links and messaging channels.

Clone Phishing

Modifying a previously delivered legitimate transmission to inject weaponized attachments/links.

Phishing Lifecycle Mechanics

Deconstruct the typical execution timeline of a multi-stage corporate credential harvesting sequence.

1

Payload Generation

The malicious actor stands up infrastructure and crafts social engineering lures designed to bypass mail filters.

2

Inbound Vector Access

The malicious message enters internal endpoints and lands directly inside the target inbox.

3

Lure Interaction

The operator interacts with structural email fields and clicks the hyperlinked redirect URL.

4

Phishing Portal Landing

The client endpoint opens a spoofed landing page mimicking a legitimate authentication server.

5

Credential Exfiltration

The target keys in usernames, passwords, or multi-factor tokens, streaming plain text logs straight to the threat server.

6

Infrastructure Compromise

The adversary leverages stolen session parameters or data points to gain persistent network access.
